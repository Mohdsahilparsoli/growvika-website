Do you need an app, or just a better website?

A simple way to decide whether your business needs a mobile app or whether a fast, mobile-friendly website is enough.

MS
Md SahilFounder, GrowVika
8 Oct 20263 min read

“Should we build an app?” is one of the most common questions we hear. Sometimes the honest answer is no — a fast mobile website does the job at a fraction of the cost. Here's how to decide.

Start with how often customers come back

Apps make sense when customers use your service repeatedly — ordering food every week, booking classes, tracking deliveries, checking accounts. If customers visit once or twice a year (like a lawyer or an interior designer), a website is almost always enough.

When a website is the better choice

  • You mainly need people to find you on Google and contact you.
  • Customers don't need to log in or save anything.
  • You want something live within a couple of weeks.
  • Your budget is limited and you'd rather invest in marketing.

When an app is worth it

  • Repeat orders or bookings — restaurants, salons, gyms, clinics.
  • Loyalty and memberships — points, plans, renewals.
  • Push notifications — reminders and offers that bring people back.
  • Device features — location tracking, camera, offline use.
  • Field teams — sales or service staff updating work from the road.

The middle path: a web app

A progressive web app (PWA) runs in the browser but can be added to the home screen like an app. It's cheaper than native apps and works on Android and iPhone. It's a great first step to test whether customers really want an app.

Tip: If you do build an app, build Android and iOS from one codebase (for example Flutter or React Native) so you don't pay twice.

A quick decision checklist

Answer yes or no:

  1. Do customers use our service more than once a month?
  2. Do they need to log in to see orders, bookings or balances?
  3. Would push reminders genuinely help them?
  4. Do we need the phone's camera, location or offline mode?
  5. Do we have the budget for ongoing updates?

Mostly yes → an app is worth exploring. Mostly no → invest in a fast website, Google Business Profile and WhatsApp first.

What a good first version includes

Keep version one focused: login, the one main action (order, book, renew), payments, notifications and a simple admin panel. Leave nice-to-have features for version two, after you see how customers use the app.

Tip: Design the main flow so it takes three taps or fewer — for example: open app → choose slot → pay.

Getting people to download it

An app nobody installs is wasted money. Promote it at the counter with a QR code, in WhatsApp messages, on receipts and on your website. Give a reason to install — app-only offers, faster booking or loyalty points.

Publishing on Play Store and App Store

You'll need developer accounts in your business name, a privacy policy, screenshots and a description. Apple's review can take longer and has stricter guidelines, so plan a few extra days before launch.

Don't forget the running costs

Apps need updates for new phone versions, store fees, hosting and ongoing fixes. Budget for monthly maintenance, not just the first build.

Key takeaways

  • Repeat usage is the main reason to build an app
  • A fast mobile website is enough for most local businesses
  • A PWA is a low-cost middle path
  • Build both platforms from one codebase
  • Plan for ongoing maintenance
